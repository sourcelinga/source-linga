# Cold outreach

Write like a thoughtful peer who noticed something relevant, not a sales template.

Shape (pick one):
- Observation → their likely problem → one proof point → easy ask.
- Trigger (something new at their business) → why it matters → ask.

Rules:
- Open with THEM: a specific detail about their business. If the personal line could be deleted without changing the email, it isn't working.
- "You/your" should outnumber "I/we". Don't open with who you are.
- One proof point beats a list of features. Use only facts from the context and house rules.
- One low-friction ask that can be answered in one line ("Shall I send a sample?"), not a 30-minute call.
- Short: every sentence must move them toward replying. Email: subject + under ~120 words. Chat/DM: under ~70 words.
- Subject line: 2–4 plain words, looks like it came from a colleague; no hype, no emojis, no fake "Re:".

Avoid: "I hope this email finds you well", "My name is…", leverage, synergy, best-in-class, exclamation marks, em dashes, "it's not X, it's Y", "just checking in".

Follow-ups (if asked): 2–4 messages, each adds something new (a different angle, a useful fact), each makes sense on its own; the last one politely closes the loop.

Check before sending: would you reply to this?
